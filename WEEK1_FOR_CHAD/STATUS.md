## 2. Where the plan was wrong

### 2.1 The seven failed emails — do not replay them

The plan says diagnose the seven failures and replay the sends. **Six of the seven were never failures.**

Every one of those six leads has an email log entry with a Resend provider ID, timestamped within sixty seconds of the lead arriving. They got their email. The `failed` label came from a *later* sequence step failing and overwriting the earlier success. Replaying them would have been the third duplicate send to those people — two already went out on 4 and 9 September during the queue recovery.

**One lead genuinely never received anything.** The 23 August lead's email address is `shortales@rocketmail` — no `.com`. The wizard accepted a domain with no top-level domain, Resend rejected it four times, and nothing classified that as permanent so the queue kept retrying something that could never succeed.

Fixed:
- An address validator that rejects exactly that shape before it costs a provider call
- Permanent-versus-transient error classification, so a dead address stops looping
- A Resend delivery webhook with Svix signature verification that fails closed
- Migration 007, applied to production: six false failure labels corrected, the one real failure now marked `invalid_address`

**On SPF, DKIM and DMARC:** all three records exist and are correctly formed. DKIM is at `resend._domainkey`, SPF and the bounce MX are on `send.ratereliefca.com`. DMARC is present at `p=none`, which is monitoring only — worth hardening to `quarantine` after you have looked at a few reports, but it was not the cause of anything.

**The delivery gate you cannot currently measure:** item 1.1's done-criterion is a test batch at 98% delivered. There were zero delivery events in the system, because nothing recorded them. "Sent" meant "Resend accepted the request". The webhook is what makes that number exist. Until you paste a signing secret into Railway and add the webhook in the Resend dashboard, the gate has no denominator.

**One decision for you.** That 23 August lead is probably reachable — `shortales@rocketmail.com` is almost certainly what they meant. I did not send to a guessed address, because a guess could reach a stranger. Your call.

### 2.2 The city pages are two files, not 154

This is the biggest finding of the night and it changes the cost of the largest block in the plan.

All 154 city pages are generated by **two template files** — `solar-savings/[city]/page.tsx` and `solar-companies/[city]/page.tsx` — fed by one data file, `src/data/cities-data.ts`, holding 77 city entries and a 12-entry utility table. There are only six hand-written pages under `/solar-savings/`, and they are the regional hubs.

What that means:
- The 71–81% duplicate copy is **one template problem**, not 154 page problems. It is fixed by branching the template's prose and enriching the data file.
- The nine per-city data fields the plan specifies (Block 5.3) are a **schema change plus a data population job**, not 37 hand-written pages.
- Block 9's merge is a **routing and data decision**, not 77 individual redirects.
- Every city page is only as correct as one field in one file. Which is exactly how Bakersfield happened.

Block 9's canary is still the right idea and the 7-day clock still matters. But the labour estimate for Blocks 9c–9f should come down a lot.
